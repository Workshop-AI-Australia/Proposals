03 · How we decide what to buildFour questions before any agent.

Not every job should be an agent. Before we build one, it has to pass four questions. A clear "no" on any of the first three means we don't build it. The fourth decides how much it's allowed to do on its own, which is how your judgement stays protected.

1

Is the input structured?

Can the work start from something consistent, like your quote form or a plan set, not a vague one-line email?

2

Are the steps definable?

Can we write down what it does, the same way each time? Your pricing is largely fixed formulas, so much of it can.

3

Is the output checkable?

Can you look at the result and tell quickly whether it's right? With the workings shown, you can.

4

What's the cost of a wrong answer?

Low and reversible: the agent acts. High or hard to undo: the agent drafts, you commit.

This is why a price never sends on its own. The agent does the legwork; you stay on the trigger.

04 · The pathOne piece at a time.

The full picture is six to eight months of work, and it's worth being straight about that up front. But you don't sign up for all of it now. We build one piece, it proves its worth, and you decide on the next. You set the pace, and you can pause between pieces. Each one stands on its own and makes the next one possible.

The first piece

Scoped together

2

The front door

The triage and "enough to quote?" gates working across your inbound, so incomplete and not-worth-it jobs are sorted before they reach you, and the gaps get chased automatically. Cleans every input downstream.

3

The pricing engine

Your formulas, glass-spec rules, and cost list turned into an agent that drafts a fully worked quote from structured line items, with the maths shown so you can check it at a glance.

4

The takeoff

The hard one

The centrepiece: reading a plan set and pulling out each element and its measurements. This is the genuinely difficult part, and the reason we build the rest first, so it has clean inputs to work from. Scoped properly once we're there.

5

ServiceM8 sync and the learning loop

Wiring it all into ServiceM8 so drafts land on the job ready for you, and feeding real outcomes (won or lost, actual hours) back in so the pricing sharpens itself over time, the way you already do by hand.

Why this order. The hard, valuable part is the takeoff. Everything before it makes that part possible by giving it clean, structured inputs to work from. Building the front door and the pricing engine first isn't the cautious option, it's the order that makes the whole thing work.
