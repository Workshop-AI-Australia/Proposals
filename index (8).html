01 · How we workTwo units a month, built to fit together.

The engagement is a monthly retainer of two units of build capacity a month. A unit is a defined piece of build work. Some pieces are one unit, some run to two or more, depending on how much is genuinely new. The two ways to take it up, by term and rate, are set out at the end.

Every piece is built against the end state from the start: the same owned data store, the same logged-record pattern, the same shared way in. Each one stands up on its own the day it ships and slots into the whole the day after, with nothing to unpick and rebuild later.

We move as fast as we can to the highest standard we can, and we hold that line on systems that touch your money.

02 · The foundationThe first month: getting the ground solid.

Before any build, the ground it stands on needs to be solid: a shared, agreed understanding across the team of how AI is used, what's off-limits, what data never goes near it, and how the tools are set up and kept. Today that understanding sits with one or two people. The foundation spreads it across the whole team.

This is a facilitated program, the first two units of the engagement. We run the process that produces the policy and gets the team using it. The binding policy itself is drafted and signed off separately. The program covers:

What the foundation includes

  • A baseline survey of how the team uses AI and how they feel about it, doubling as a tool audit of what's in use and how it could connect.
  • A company-wide guide, built with the team: approved tools, what data never goes in, who's accountable, how shared things are set up and kept. Co-created, so it's followed.
  • The project-setup standard and the rules for where skills live and who can change them.
  • Team training, built to be testable, so absorption is something you can see, not assume.
  • A change process so the guide stays current as tools and rules shift.
  • A closing reassessment, the opening survey re-run, so the shift is visible.

What it isn't

  • A repeat of the discovery you've already had. That work is done; this builds on it.
  • A document that sits in a drawer. The team shapes it and learns to use it.
  • The binding legal policy itself, which is drafted and signed off separately.
  • A one-off. The change process keeps it alive past week four.

Why first. The foundation is the base every later build sits on, and the fastest win in the whole engagement. It also resolves the two patterns that run through everything the review found: work only one person can run, and good practice that never spreads because there's no shared standard.

03 · The sequenceWhat we'd build, in order.

After the foundation, the work runs roughly in this order. The order isn't locked, it firms up as we learn, and each piece earns its place. Unit counts are indicative: they tell you the shape of the work, not a fixed quote.

1

The foundation

2 units · month one

The governance program above. Survey, the team-wide guide, tool mapping, training, the change process, and a closing reassessment.

2

The project index

~1 unit

A self-maintaining index for the project documents, built into the project-setup standard so every project inherits it. High reach, used daily, and a strong early win.

3

The daily reconciliation check

~2 units

Rebuilt as a bounded check with its own logged history, putting the financial safety net back in place and setting the pattern, owned store, logged record, exceptions to a person, that the later builds reuse.

4

The month-end review

~1 unit

Built as an extension of the daily check rather than a separate system. Its running record makes month-end fast.

5

The invoice pipeline

~4–6 units

Invoice routing that places invoices and learns from corrections; the approval app migrated off Lovable onto infrastructure you own with a corrections log; and the two joined into one flow, with a single clean-pass measure across the whole pipeline.

6

Watch and shift

ongoing

Bob's jobs move to native tooling as the cloud-scheduling and messaging pieces mature. The larger system assembles from these components over time, rather than as one large release.

04 · Time and costWhat it adds up to.

At two units a month, the work above breaks down like this. We'd ask for a six-month minimum term, because that's the runway the build genuinely needs and it lets each piece compound on the last.

Best case

  • Twelve units, delivered over six months.
  • The full scoped sequence, foundation through the invoice pipeline.
  • The minimum term and the fastest the work could land.

Realistic ceiling

  • Up to eighteen units, around nine months, billed at the same monthly rate as the work continues.
  • Several builds are first-of-their-kind here, in a multi-entity business with real data complexity.
  • We work to the faster end, and review pace with you every month.
  • You're never committed past the six-month minimum.

Plainly. Best case is six months. Based on what we know today, it could run to around nine. We'll move as quickly as we can without cutting corners on systems that touch your money, and you'll see the pace every month rather than at the end.
